Chapter 4: Wormholes – The Hidden Pathways in Space

4.1 What is a Wormhole?

  • A wormhole is a tunnel created from a dead black hole that forms a fast-moving vortex beyond the speed of light.
  • This idea is based on theory — not yet proven, but it follows logic inspired by Hawking radiation and cosmic compression.

4.2 How Wormholes Form

  • When a black hole dies from Hawking radiation, it slowly decays and releases radiation.
  • In most cases, the black hole may vanish, scattering radiation across space.
  • In extremely rare cases, the dead black hole might implode and form a small explosion — a nova.
  • This explosion ejects a vortex tunnel spinning faster than the speed of light.
  • Because it’s faster than light, the core of the tunnel becomes hidden in a separate dimension-like space.
  • To us, it appears as a glowing sphere. The fast inner vortex is invisible, while the slower outer edges are visible.

4.3 The Size and Reach of Wormholes

  • The distance a wormhole can reach depends on the size of the explosion:
    • A nova = billions of light-years,
    • A supernova = trillions to quadrillions,
    • A hypernova (extremely rare) = nearly the full width of the universe.

4.4 How Wormholes Work

  • The spinning tunnel acts like a forward current — weaker than a black hole but strong enough to pull objects in.
  • Because the vortex is faster than light, it carries objects through time-space at incredible speed.
  • However, wormholes don’t teleport instantly like in movies — they still have travel time.
  • For example, a journey that takes millions of years could take only months or years through a wormhole.

4.5 Why Wormholes Are Temporary

  • The spin energy that holds a wormhole together is not infinite.
  • As time passes — perhaps trillions of years — the energy fades and the wormhole collapses.
